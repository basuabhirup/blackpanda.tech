1. White-Label Client Automation for Lead Handling

Agencies often manage leads across multiple clients, platforms, and funnels. Over time, this becomes fragmented and manual.

What we typically build:

automated lead intake from forms, ads, chatbots, and landing pages

enrichment and validation of lead data

routing logic based on rules or context

handoff to CRMs, email tools, or internal dashboards

How it’s maintained:

workflows orchestrated using make.com or n8n

logic that agencies can inspect and adjust later

The result is a system agencies can resell under their own brand, without needing to explain the technical details to clients.

2. Internal Automation for Agency Operations

Many agencies focus on client automation while running their own operations manually.

Typical internal use cases include:

onboarding new clients

campaign setup checklists

internal reporting and alerts

syncing data between tools used by different teams

These systems usually start small and evolve over time, rather than being “fully automated” from day one.

3. Client-Facing AI Assistants (White-Label)

Some agency clients want chat-based experiences, but not generic AI bots.

We help agencies offer:

website assistants for lead qualification or FAQs

onboarding assistants for SaaS or service businesses

internal knowledge assistants for client teams

The emphasis is on:

narrow scope

predictable behavior

clear boundaries on what the assistant can and can’t do

All assistants are delivered white-label and designed to integrate cleanly into existing funnels.

4. Workflow Rescue & Cleanup

Agencies often inherit fragile automations built quickly or by multiple vendors.

Common situations:

automations breaking silently

duplicated logic across tools

unclear ownership or documentation

workflows that no one wants to touch anymore

We help by:

auditing existing workflows

simplifying logic

rebuilding critical paths using make.com or n8n

documenting systems so they’re easier to maintain

This is often a starting point before new automation is added.

5. Custom Tools for Agency-Specific Needs

Sometimes agencies need small, focused tools that don’t justify a full product build.

Examples include:

internal dashboards

lightweight admin panels

client-specific reporting views

tools that sit between existing platforms

These are usually frontend-heavy, practical, and built to solve a very specific problem.

6. Ongoing White-Label Technical Support

For some agencies, the main need is not a single build, but ongoing technical continuity.

This can include:

maintaining existing automations

extending workflows as client needs evolve

acting as a quiet technical backend for new service offerings

The agency stays client-facing.
The technical work stays behind the scenes.
